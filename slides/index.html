The problem

Confidence is not accuracy, and nothing usually checks.

  • A language model will tell you it is 90% sure.
  • Nothing scores that claim.
  • Nothing changes if it turns out to be wrong.
  • So the number is decoration, and the size behind it is a guess.

Saadhak makes the claim falsifiable, scores it, and lets that score decide how much money it may risk.

What it trades

Defined risk, every time.

  • Iron condors and vertical spreads — they profit when the market stays inside a range.
  • one day to expiry, on liquid index products
  • never naked, never a market order
  • the maximum loss is known before the order goes in

What earns the size

The mechanism the whole design rests on.

  • Every review states a probability. The witness resolves it against what happened.
  • 90% claimed and right half the time means the budget is cut
  • the engine sets the band, so a wider guess cannot buy a better score

Brier 0.248 over 40 · says 48%, right 57% → 0.70× of full size

It knows when to stop

  • Two consecutive stop-outs block new entries in that name.
  • SPY and QQQ correlate at 0.91 — one position, not two.
  • A 3% daily loss halts entries; 6% flattens everything.
  • exits keep running through a halt — it never traps a position

The results, honestly

  • Four positions across two sessions. Three stopped out when the index trended.
  • the account finished at $99,212 — down $788

Two sessions is not a track record. What this demonstrates is a mechanism.

What it rests on

Stated plainly, because it matters.
